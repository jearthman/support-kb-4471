parser: the shared parser reads dates month first

This covers 374 reports. Every tenant on file writes the day first. The shared parser reads 07/04 as 4 July instead of 7 April, so the day and the month trade places: “Our 7 April reminder went out on 4 July.” This is what #4471 describes, and its queued fix is for this problem.

A few of these reports name no dates (a request for a “day first” setting, “October bookings came through for January”). They are counted under their tenant’s cause, the way causes.ts places every report.

hint: the booking-v3 form tells day-first tenants to type month first

This covers 33 reports, all through the booking form of the tenants that run booking-v3. That form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People type what the hint says, month first, and the form reads the date the tenant’s way, day first. The symptom is the same swap, so the subject lines match the parser reports and the merge hid it. A parser fix does not touch the hint; the form has to print the tenant’s order, or read the order it asks for.

one-day: bookings move by one day

This covers 5 reports: the ones the desk itself read as a one-day move (desk-notes.json). Nothing in them is swapped. The date lands one day off (“Tuesday 6 October showed as Monday”), and one meeting landed on different days for clients in Lisbon and Toronto. That points at time zone handling: a date with no time moved through a clock and back. They were footnoted under parser because of their tenant; they need their own fix.

Why the merge hid them

  • The merge rule matches subject lines (decision 0001). The subjects say “wrong date”, not why, so all three problems share them.
  • Intake tags are a guess from the reporter’s browser. The table counts how many reports in each problem intake tagged as something else.
  • A footnote is not a ticket. The one-day moves were noticed and then counted under the wrong cause.

Lessons

  • Merge on cause, not on subject. Read a sample of each big ticket before marking it known.
  • When a report does not fit its ticket, give it its own ticket, not a footnote.
