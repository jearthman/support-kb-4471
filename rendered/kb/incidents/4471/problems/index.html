How a report finds its problem

  1. One id, one report. The export’s pages overlap, so a repeated row counts once.
  2. A report the desk read as a one-day move (desk-notes.json) is one-day. Nothing in it is swapped, so it leaves its tenant’s cause.
  3. Any other report goes to its cause by causes.ts, unchanged: from a tenant on booking-v3 and sent through the form, hint; from any other tenant on file, parser.
  4. Intake’s tag is not used. It is a guess from the reporter’s browser, and the table counts how often it disagrees.

parser: the shared parser reads dates month first

This covers 374 reports from 11 tenants. Every tenant on file writes the day first, and the shared parser reads 07/04 as 4 July, so the day and the month trade places: “Our 7 April reminder went out on 4 July.”

Engineering closed this as fixed on 5 October with fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales. One check before anyone relies on it: harbour.dental writes en-GB, which puts the day first but is not an EU locale. If the fix keeps a list of EU locales, it misses them. causes.ts asks the platform instead of keeping a list.

hint: the booking-v3 form prints the month first

This covers 33 reports, all sent through the form, from the 3 tenants on booking-v3. The form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People type what it says, and the form reads the day first. The parser fix does not touch this.

Engineering will not fix it: the booking-v3 form is retired. The tenant directory still lists these tenants on booking-v3, so the reports stop only when they move off it.

one-day: the date lands one day off

This covers 5 reports, the ones the desk itself read as a one-day move. Nothing in them is swapped. “Tuesday 6 October showed as Monday”, and one meeting landed on Tuesday for a client in Lisbon and on Monday for a client in Toronto. That points at time zones: a date with no time of day passed through a clock and came back a day early. The desk footnoted them under parser because of their tenant. Neither the parser fix nor the form touches them, and before this page they had no ticket.

What #4471 shows

  • A subject line says that a date is wrong, not why. Every problem here came in under the same subjects, so merging on subject put them in one ticket.
  • A footnote is not a ticket. The one-day moves were noticed, then counted under a cause they do not have.
  • A closed ticket can still hold an open problem. Close the tickets of the problems, not the merge.
